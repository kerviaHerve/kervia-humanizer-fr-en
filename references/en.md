# English: editing decisions

- Keep the established `en-GB` or `en-US` spelling, register and authorial distance. Official names and quotations keep their exact forms.
- Remove a staged entrance or closer that adds nothing: “Let’s dive in”, “Here’s the thing”, “Let that sink in”. Keep a real warning, objection or consequential conclusion.
- “Not just X, but Y” is a problem only when the contrast is empty. A loan really differs from a grant. Three distinct required documents remain three documents.
- Examine clusters of inflated vocabulary in context. Keep technical uses of robust, leverage, model or ecosystem. A less common synonym is not an improvement by itself.
- Recover known actors and actions from abstract noun chains and participial trailers. Never add a team, cause, guarantee or measurement to sound concrete. Keep an appropriate passive.
- Protect `may`, `can`, `should`, `must`, `must not`, `only`, `unless`, `at least` and `up to`, including their scope. Association is not causation; a percentage reduction is not the same as percentage points.
- Contractions, heading case and smart quotes are publication choices, not humanity signals. Do not impose slang, sentence fragments or a compound-hyphen rule without checking context.
- Repeated technical names may be clearer than changing terminology. Keep genuine uncertainty, humour and first-person material; invent none for the writer.
- Adjust rhythm to the thought. Do not replace repeated symmetry with deliberate randomness. A good passage may remain unchanged.
